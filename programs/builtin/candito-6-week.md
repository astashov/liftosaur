A free 6-week powerlifting program by Jonnie Candito, based on an upper/lower split. Each week has a different focus: first muscular conditioning, then hypertrophy, then hard sets of 4-6, then hard triples, and finally one top set of 1-4 reps with 97.5% - that one sets your new 1RM. Squat, bench press and deadlift are percentage-based, and on upper days there are three additional back and shoulder accessories. It's for intermediate lifters who know their maxes and want to increase them in a short block.

## Origin & Philosophy

Jonnie Candito made this program in 2013. Back then Jonnie was 21, a natural lifter, weighed 179lb and squatted about 3x bodyweight. It was released for free on the Candito Training HQ site, with a [PDF description](https://web.archive.org/web/20171010194828/http://www.canditotraininghq.com:80/app/download/911765404/Candito%206%20Week%20Strength%20Program.pdf) and Excel spreadsheet. Both were last updated in November 2013. As Jonnie put it, the program "isn't a creation, rather a formal description of how I train".

The core ideas:

- **Periodization, even for beginners.** Each week has a different goal, so you don't grind the same 5x5 until you stall. As Candito puts it: "Plateaus are best handled by preventing them in the first place!"
- **Each block prepares you for the next one.** The high-rep weeks build muscle, and that muscle carries over to the weeks with higher weights.
- **Upper/lower split.** You come in fresh and do 3-4 sets of bench, so you never do "the 18th chest set". Upper body recovers faster, so you train it 2-3 days a week, and squat and deadlift - 1-2 days.
- **Minimal leg days.** There're no required leg accessories at all. Candito says over 90% of his workouts were "squat, deadlift and leave".
- **Short, focused workouts.** Most of them take 45-60 minutes. He wants you to "come in the gym on a mission", hit the numbers, and go home.

## Who It's For

- **Experience level**: Intermediate, in practice. Candito says periodization is fine for novices too, but Lift Vault and many reviewers recommend at least a year of steady barbell training. If you're still able to add weight each workout, and the workouts feel way easier than they should, Candito himself recommends running a linear program instead.
- **Prerequisites**: You need to know your 1RMs for squat, bench press and deadlift, or at least have a good estimate of them. All main lift weights are based on them.
- **Primary goal**: Strength on squat, bench press and deadlift. You'll build some muscle in weeks 1-2, but this is a strength program.
- **Cut/bulk**: Candito recommends eating at maintenance, maybe a slight calorie surplus, with plenty of protein. He doesn't recommend running it in a deficit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Built-in max test each round - the top set in week 5 gives you a new 1RM, then the next round starts with updated numbers
- Auto-regulation on missed reps - if you fail a set, the max goes down by 2.5%, which means a single bad day won't wreck the rest of the block
- Lower body days are short - around 30 minutes of squat and deadlift, often just 6 working sets
- Squat and deadlift progress is where the program really shines - the majority of Reddit reviews report solid gains on both
- Free, and you can still get the original PDF and spreadsheet through the Web Archive

**Cons**

- Bench press is a weak spot. You bench 1-3 times a week, but there aren't many sets and intensity goes up and down, so many people stall on it after several cycles
- Going from high-volume weeks to high-intensity weeks is rough. One reviewer ran the program 3 times, called the switch from week 1-2 to week 4-5 "brutal", and missed reps in week 4 twice
- Upper days take about 60 minutes in week 1 - 16 working sets, and 12 of them are accessories
- There are no direct arm, ab or hamstring exercises among the required ones. In week 1, the only hamstring work is the 4 deadlift sets. Candito leaves that for the optional slots
- The number of days per week isn't fixed: 5 days in week 1-2, 4 days in week 3-4, 3 days in week 5. So it doesn't really fit into a fixed Mon/Wed/Fri schedule
- The whole program is short. You only spend 1-2 weeks in each block, and some people finish week 5 with a lower projected max than they started with

## Program Structure

- **Split**: Upper/Lower. Lower days are squat and deadlift (or a deadlift variation). Upper days are bench press and 3 accessories.
- **Periodization**: This is block periodization compressed into 6 weeks, with a different focus each week:
  - Week 1 - Muscular conditioning: squat and deadlift with 70-80%, for 6-8 reps, 3 upper days, and bench max reps with 80% on the last day
  - Week 2 - Hypertrophy: squat max-rep sets (up to 10 reps) and short-rest triples, a deadlift variation for 3x8, and bench max reps by the end of the week
  - Week 3 - Linear max overload: sets of 4-6 (3-6 for deadlift), with 85% plus 5-10lb, and 87.5% on deadlift
  - Week 4 - Heavy weight acclimation: triples around 87.5-90%, in 5lb steps, then singles and doubles with 95%
  - Week 5 - Intense strength: a single top set of 1-4 reps with 97.5% for each lift
  - Week 6 - Deload, test or skip
- **Schedule**: Fixed weekly. The order of days changes from week to week (e.g., week 1 is lower, upper, upper, lower, upper, and week 2 is lower, upper, lower, upper, upper).

## Exercise Selection & Rationale

- **[{Squat}], [{Bench Press}], [{Deadlift}]** - the core of the program. Candito says the max lifts should stay as the foundation, even for advanced lifters. You could swap deadlift for a snatch grip deadlift or a deficit deadlift (just stick with the same variation for the whole program), and bench press for a dumbbell bench. If you're a bodybuilder, incline bench is ok occasionally, but he doesn't suggest it for strength. And replacing the squat itself? His answer: "What's wrong with you? Trying to replace the squat?"
- **[{Stiff Leg Deadlift}]** - the "deadlift variation" slot in weeks 2-4. Candito allows stiff-legged, snatch grip, deficit and paused deadlifts for it, done with moderate effort. It adds volume for the lower body posterior chain and hamstrings without another hard deadlift, since squats are the main hard part of that day anyway.
- **[{Bent Over One Arm Row}]** - the horizontal rowing slot. Candito lists dumbbell rows first, for practical reasons. Barbell rows and machine rows work too.
- **[{Overhead Press, Dumbbell}]** - the shoulder slot. By default, Candito's spreadsheet uses standing dumbbell press. Seated dumbbell press and military press are ok too.
- **[{Pull Up}]** - the vertical pulling slot. Candito wants weighted pull-ups, but you could do weighted chin-ups and lat pulldowns instead. The code starts with 0lb of added weight.

Why no triceps accessory? Candito's take: your triceps get plenty of work from bench and overhead press, and sore triceps hurt the next bench day. Biceps get way less from rows. In the original PDF the third accessory slot was biceps (dumbbell curl first), but his spreadsheet replaced it with a second back exercise.

Pick accessories you can progress over time, since you'll do them every upper day.

**Optional exercises** aren't in the program text. Candito gives you 2 optional slots per day (except week 3, there're none there). Use those for light isolation exercises - 4 sets of 8-12 reps, counting the warm-up, and rest 1-2 minutes after each set:

- Upper workouts: [{Lateral Raise}], incline chest fly, single-arm triceps extension, cable curls, [{Preacher Curl}], [{Skullcrusher}], [{Hammer Curl}]
- Lower workouts, hypertrophy: calf raises, [{Lying Leg Curl}], [{Leg Extension}], glute isolation exercises, single-leg press, single-leg curl
- Lower workouts, explosive: box jumps, [{Jump Squat}], power cleans, full-depth squat jumps, med ball throws. You always do these in sets of 4 reps.

Candito's advice: if you're a bodybuilder, go with the hypertrophy exercises. If you're doing it for athletics and want speed, go with the explosive exercises. Everybody who's neither may mix both. Or you may not do any of these at all - Candito mentioned that most of his own workouts didn't have them.

## Set & Rep Scheme

- **Main lifts**: % of 1RM. Each week the volume goes down and the intensity goes up. Week 1 is 4x8 with 70%, and by week 5 it's just a single top set with 97.5%.
- **Max-reps (MR) sets**: bench press on the last upper day of weeks 1 and 2. You do as many reps as you can with 80%, and then with 80% minus 5lb.
- **MR10 sets**: squat in week 2. It's max reps, but you stop when you get to 10. Those sets decide the back-off volume, and also whether the max goes down.
- **Rep ranges like 4-6 or 1-4**: do as many reps as you can within the range. The low end is the minimum, and the high end is the cap.
- **Accessories**: no percentages here. 6-12 reps for weeks 1 and 4, 6-10 for week 2, and then sets of 6-8 for weeks 3 and 5.
- **Deadlift variation**: 8 reps for weeks 2-3, and 6 reps for week 4.

The spreadsheet often has weights like "80% + 5lb" or "90% - 5lb". Mostly it's "one plate step heavier than last time" - e.g. the second squat day of the week is 5lb heavier than the first one. The program shows the base percentage (80%), and when you start the workout, the app adds or subtracts the 5lb steps. The step size is the `step` variable in the `main` template's progression - if you lift in kg, change it from 5lb to 2.5kg before your first workout, like Candito's spreadsheet does.

## Progressive Overload

The weights come from your 1RM, so progress means the 1RM goes up. It changes in two ways.

**Week 5 projection (success).** You do 1-4 reps with 97.5% once, on each exercise. Then the app updates the 1RM based on that, using Candito's multipliers:

- 1 rep: Candito gives no multiplier here, so the app keeps 1RM the same.
- 2 reps: weight x 1.03
- 3 reps: weight x 1.06
- 4 reps: weight x 1.09

For example, 300lb x 3 on squat gives a new 1RM of 318lb. Week 6 and the next cycle use it.

**Missed reps (failed sets).** If you miss a required rep in Squat, Bench Press or Deadlift (i.e. you do less reps than the lower end of the range), the 1RM for that lift goes down by 2.5%. You don't repeat the workout. You just finish as much as you can, and keep going with the lower max. The MR10 squats have separate rules:

- Week 2, first Squat day: if you do less than 8 reps, the max goes down by 2.5%. You still do all 5 triples.
- Week 2, second Squat day: if you do 10 reps, you do 10 back-off triples, if 8-9 reps - 8 triples, if 7 reps - 5 triples. If you do less than 7, you skip the back-off sets, and the max goes down by 2.5%. After you log the MR10 set, the app will delete the sets you don't need. The back-off weight is the top set weight minus 10lb.

So, 2.5% is taken from a spreadsheet. PDF says 5% for this same rule, but both of them are dated November 2013. I picked spreadsheet's number, because it's a thing that actually generates workouts.

**Accessories.** Candito says you should increase the weight gradually over the 6 weeks, and lower it when you're close to failing the reps. In the program, the rows, dumbbell press and pull-ups add 5lb after every 3 workouts where you hit all reps. The deadlift variation adds 5lb after every 2.

**Deload.** On Week 6 you repeat Week 1, but skip the last upper workout and use the updated 1RMs. If you miss reps on Week 6, it won't affect the max.

## How Long to Run It / What Next

Candito intended the program to be run over and over: "This program is absolutely designed for repeated use." So, you can just repeat it cycle after cycle, while the week 5 weights continue to go up.

For week 6 the spreadsheet gives 3 options:

1. Skip the 6th week, and just switch to the 1st day of the 1st week with the projected maxes
2. Do the deload with the projected maxes (that's how the program currently does it)
3. Test the actual 1RMs on the 6th week, and then either deload or start over from the 1st week

If you test, Candito suggests adding a 7th week as a deload.

It's time to switch when the week 5 projected max doesn't increase for 2 consecutive runs of the program, or when bench stalls but squat and deadlift keep progressing. At that point:

- [Juggernaut Method](/programs/juggernaut-method) - 16 weeks of wave-like rep schemes with AMRAP sets, and way more volume per exercise
- [Calgary Barbell 16-Week](/programs/calgary-barbell-16-week) - a complete meet prep, and you bench 4x/week there, which makes it a good choice if your bench is lagging
- [Sheiko #29-32](/programs/sheiko-29-32) - high frequency and a ton of submaximal sets, good if you respond well to practice

If Candito feels too advanced, [Texas Method](/programs/texasmethod) or [GZCLP](/programs/gzclp) are simpler options.

## Equipment Needed

- A bar with plates, a squat rack and a bench
- A set of dumbbells (for rows and overhead press)
- A chin-up bar, and also a belt with a chain to hang plates on for the weighted ones
- A plyo box, but only if you do the explosive optional exercises

So, if you have a home gym, you're good - all the required exercises use free weights. If you can't do pull-ups yet, do [{Lat Pulldown}] instead, and if you exercise at home, [{Pull Up, Band}] is also fine. For the machine rowing exercises, you could replace them with [{Bent Over One Arm Row}] or [{Bent Over Row}].

## Rest Times

Candito wants you to rest long enough to be fully recovered before each hard set: "3-8 minutes in between sets will generally be optimal." For bench press it's probably closer to 3, and on squat days you may even go over 8. This is how it looks in the program:

- Squat, Deadlift: 4 min (3 min for light Deadlift sets in week 5)
- Bench Press: 3 min
- Squat triples after MR10 sets in week 2: 60 seconds - that's from the spreadsheet
- Deadlift variation: 2.5 min
- Accessories: 2 min. Candito doesn't list a separate number for them, and with the 3-8 min rule, upper days would be way over 60 min
- Optional exercises: 1-2 min

To save time on upper days, Candito suggests supersets: one set of bench, then one set of rows, and so on. Then shoulders paired with the third accessory.

## How to Pick Starting Weights

The program needs your **real 1RMs** for squat, bench press and deadlift - not a training max. Enter them when you start the program.

- If you tested your max recently, just take that number.
- If not, estimate it from some recent hard set. For example, a 5-rep set with 250lb is about a 290lb max.
- It's ok to be a bit conservative here. The week 5 test will correct it upward anyway. Starting with a max that's higher than it should be is what people usually get wrong. If you miss reps on week 1 or 2, the max was probably set higher than it should be.

For the accessories, choose a weight you can do for all prescribed reps with 1-2 reps left in the tank. In the code, it's 30lb for the dumbbell row, 25lb dumbbells for the press, bodyweight for pull-ups, and 115lb for stiff-legged deadlift. The lower-rep weeks use more weight though: row is 35lb and press is 30lb in weeks 3 and 5, and stiff-legged deadlift is 125lb in week 4. You can change them on the first workout.

Warm-ups: Candito recommends making warm-ups short. Begin with light weights, and then do sets of 1-4 reps till you get to your target weight. So, before a hard squat set, you'd do something like: 135 x 10, 225 x 4, 315 x 4, 375 x 3, 405 x 2, 425 x 1. On bench days with lots of sets and reps, 1-2 warm-up sets are enough.

## Common Modifications

- **More Bench Press volume**: the most popular fix. Some lifters add 2-3 sets to each Bench Press day, add close-grip or paused Bench Press as optional exercises, or do [Calgary Barbell 16-Week](/programs/calgary-barbell-16-week) style Bench Press training. Lift Vault suggests combining it with Candito's separate Advanced Bench Program.
- **Biceps instead of a second lats exercise**: that's how the original PDF version does it. Replace [{Pull Up}] with [{Bicep Curl}].
- **Different deadlift variation**: replace [{Stiff Leg Deadlift}] with [{Romanian Deadlift}], [{Deficit Deadlift}] or a paused deadlift.
- **Lighter shoulder training**: a reviewer found 12-rep overhead press sets to be a lot. A popular replacement is to do [{Overhead Press}] with more weight, but for fewer reps.
- **Abs**: you can do them on any day you want, except the day before a leg day. Candito's reasoning: when your abs are sore, big squats and deadlifts shift the load to the lower back.

### Is the Candito 6 Week Program good for beginners?

It depends. Candito says it works for newer people as well: "periodization is optimal for beginners as well". But most reviewers, and Lift Vault, consider it intermediate, so it's for people with a year or more of experience and known 1RMs. If you can put more weight on the bar every workout, a linear program like GZCLP will take you further for now. Candito himself recommends his linear program to novices who find these workouts not challenging enough.

### How many days a week is Candito 6 Week?

The number of workouts changes from week to week. On week 1 and 2 you do 5 workouts (3 upper, 2 lower), on week 3 and 4 it's 4 workouts, and on week 5 it's just 3. Then, on week 6 there's optional deload, with 4 workouts.

### What does MR10 mean in Candito?

MR10 means max reps, but not more than 10. I.e. you do the max reps you can with that weight, and stop once you hit 10. In week 2, the number of reps decides how many back-off triples you do, and whether the max drops by 2.5%.

### What happens if I fail a set on Candito?

Reduce 1RM for that lift by 2.5% and continue. That's the number from the spreadsheet, the PDF says 5% though. No need to repeat the workout - just finish what you can, and the lower max will be used starting from the following workout. If you fail early in the program, it usually means the starting max was set higher than it should be.

### How do I calculate my new max after week 5?

Take the weight of your week 5 top set and multiply it by 1.03 if you got 2 reps, 1.06 for 3 reps, or 1.09 for 4 reps. Candito gives no rule for a single. The app keeps your max the same. In Liftosaur the app does this for you when you finish the workout.

### Why is bench progress slow on the Candito 6 week program?

Bench Press only gets 1-4 working sets per workout, and its intensity goes higher or lower every week. Squat and Deadlift respond well to that, but Bench Press usually wants more volume. Many lifters add more Bench Press sets, or run a separate Bench Press program alongside Candito's lower days.

### Do I have to do the optional exercises?

No, according to Candito you can skip all the optional ones and just do the required exercises - 4 on upper days, 2 on lower days. That's how he did it himself most of the time. The optional slots are there for weak points, and for some extra arms and legs work.

### What should I do in week 6 of Candito?

For week 6 there are 3 options. You can skip it, and just go to a new round with the projected maxes. You can do a deload, with week 1 workouts but without the last upper day. Or you can test the actual 1RMs, and then either deload or jump straight into a new round.
