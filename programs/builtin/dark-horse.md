A free 12-week strength and conditioning program by strongman and coach Brian Alsruhe. Four days a week, one main lift per day. You work up to a 5, 3 or 1 rep max on a variation of that lift, do 3 volume sets at 80% of whatever you hit, then 10 minutes of speed work on the opposite lift. Everything heavy is done in giant sets with a pull, core work and a bit of conditioning, so you get stronger and leaner at the same time.

## Origin & Philosophy

Brian Alsruhe released Dark Horse for free in May 2018, as a [21-minute whiteboard video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G1SYuO8XWRM) on Brian's YouTube channel. It was the fourth free program from him, and it was also the template for the e-book he was working on back then. "Dark Horse" - because, as he put it, "it has a habit of making strength and PRs kind of show up when you didn't expect them."

It's kind of a mix of conjugate, linear, rotating percentage schemes and DUP periodization - as Alsruhe puts it, "I take what's useful and I discard what's not." It mostly boils down to 3 main principles:

* **You set the load each day.** You build up to a rep max on the day, and then the volume sets use 80% of that number. If it's a good day, volume sets get bigger weights. If it's a bad day, they get smaller weights. There's no training max to calculate, and "it doesn't matter whether you've been lifting for three months or 23 years."
* **Variations fix weak points.** For the top sets you use a variation of each lift, and you pick it based on where you're weak (e.g. rack pulls if you lose deadlifts on lockout). The competition lifts themselves are trained in speed sets with reduced weights, because "lifting is definitely a perishable skill."
* **Everything is a giant set.** Between the top attempts, you do a pull or an opposite movement, a core exercise, and a short conditioning piece. Alsruhe wants to be "athletic, be a dangerous lifter, not just a guy who looks good and can't do anything". That's why there are giant sets - they keep body fat low and work capacity high.

He's pretty upfront that it's hard: "it is brutally brutally hard... I do not write programs for the average person."

## Who It's For

* **Experience level**: Alsruhe says it's suitable for beginners up to advanced lifters, because the load is self-regulated. But realistically it's best for intermediates - you need good technique to build up to true rep maxes on 12 different variations, and the volume is a lot for someone in their first months of training.
* **Prerequisites**: You need to know your 1RM (or a decent estimate) for [{Squat}], [{Bench Press}], [{Deadlift}] and [{Overhead Press}], since the speed sets use percentages of those. You also should be OK with being out of breath during strength training.
* **Primary goal**: Strength, and on top of that - tons of conditioning and work capacity. Alsruhe's promise: "you will drop body fat, you'll get stronger and you'll get bigger."
* **Cut/bulk**: OK on maintenance or a slight surplus. Reviewers dropped several pounds of fat and still held on to their strength. Doing a hard cut with that much volume is asking for trouble - people reported almost fainting during assistance exercises.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

* Self-regulating - volume is 80% of the weight you hit that day. So on a bad day volume gets lighter automatically, and you don't get stuck on some fixed percentage
* Conditioning is baked in, no need for separate cardio days - giant sets with ball slams, swings and battle ropes keep your heart rate high during the whole strength block
* Main exercises are trained 2 ways a week: heavy (rep max on a variation) and fast (speed sets on the competition version 2-3 days later)
* Rotating 5/3/1 rep maxes mean every main exercise gets a high, medium and low intensity week within every 3-week block, so there's no week that completely crushes you
* Easier on the joints for many people: you only go max on the competition versions in the last block, and the variations spread the stress around
* Free, and the author explains all the logic in a single video

**Cons**

* Workouts are long. The author himself plans for 75-90 minutes, and reviewers said it often took even longer, since you have to set up 3-4 stations for giant sets in a busy gym
* It's very hard, especially the first wave. Several reviewers had to skip assistance exercises on some days, and one said "about 5 weeks in I didn't think I would be able to finish it"
* You often won't hit the target reps on the 80% volume sets (e.g. 9, 8, 8 instead of 10, 10, 10). That's allowed - you just split those into smaller sets - but it can still feel like failing
* Strength gains are mixed. Some people got 50+ lb on squat and deadlift, others didn't move on bench and deadlift at all. The program is designed for all-round strength and conditioning, and won't peak you for a powerlifting meet
* You need a lot of equipment and space: barbell, kettlebell, medicine ball, battle ropes, dip and pull-up stations, and also bands or chains for speed training if you have those
* There's no direct biceps training, so add some if you care about arms

## Program Structure

* **Split**: 4 days, each with its own main lift - Deadlift Day, Overhead Press Day, Squat Day and Bench Day. Alsruhe prefers to start a week with deadlifts, and to have 24-48 hours between workout days (e.g. Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday).
* **Periodization**: 3 blocks of 3 weeks, followed by a deload week and 2 testing weeks, so it's 12 weeks total. Each block uses different variations, and rep max targets rotate 5/3/1 between days - so within a week, a day is either high, medium or low intensity.
* **Each workout day** goes like this:
  1. **Strength** - gradually build to a rep max on a block's variation, via giant sets
  2. **Volume** - 3 sets with 80% of today's top set, also giant sets
  3. **Speed work (dynamic effort)** - 10 minutes, a set of 3 each minute, on the opposite competition lift
  4. **Assistance** - around 20 minutes for weak points
  5. **Conditioning** - 10 minutes, optional

The rep max rotation looks like this. It repeats in each wave:

| Lift     | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 |
| -------- | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| Deadlift | 3RM    | 5RM    | 1RM    |
| OHP      | 1RM    | 3RM    | 5RM    |
| Squat    | 5RM    | 1RM    | 3RM    |
| Bench    | 3RM    | 5RM    | 1RM    |

## Exercise Selection & Rationale

**Strength variations.** Alsruhe recommends to "intently choose exercises to address your weak points", and then stick with a variation for the whole 3-week block. Reviewers who were changing variations every week got a reply that the program isn't supposed to be run like that. Here we use some reasonable setup, but feel free to replace those:

* **Deadlift Day**: [{Deficit Deadlift}] (weeks 1-3) to build speed from the floor, [{Trap Bar Deadlift}] (weeks 4-6) to lift larger weights with less stress on the lower spine, and then regular [{Deadlift}] (weeks 7-9) to prepare for testing
* **Overhead Press Day**: [{Push Press, Barbell}] to move more weight than you can with a strict press, [{Seated Overhead Press}] so legs and torso don't help the lift, and then strict [{Overhead Press}]
* **Squat Day**: [{Box Squat}] for depth and hip drive, [{Front Squat}] to keep the torso vertical and hit quads, and then regular [{Squat}]
* **Bench Day**: [{Bench Press Close Grip}] for triceps and lockout, [{Incline Bench Press}] for upper chest and shoulders, and then regular [{Bench Press}]

Alsruhe's own examples are more strongman-flavored: rack pulls, log and axle presses, continental clean and press. If you have the equipment, use them.

**Giant set partners.** Each big attempt gets paired with the opposite exercise, the abs exercise and a quick conditioning piece. For Overhead Press Day, it's exactly what the original describes - weighted pull-ups, dragon flags and medicine ball slams. Other days follow the same structure:

* **Deadlift Day** - [{Goblet Squat}], [{Ab Wheel}], and [{Ball Slams}]
* **Overhead Press Day** - [{Pull Up}], [{Dragon Flag}], and [{Battle Ropes}]
* **Squat Day** - [{Romanian Deadlift, Barbell}], [{Hanging Leg Raise}], and [{Kettlebell Swing}]
* **Bench Day** - [{Bent Over Row}], [{Plank}], and [{Ball Slams}]

Keep the partner exercises light enough that your next big attempt doesn't suffer. They're only there to keep you warm and moving between the big lifts - it shouldn't become a second workout.

**Speed work** is always done on the competition lift, with the usual grip, stance and cues. It's paired with the "opposite" lift: on Deadlift Day you do speed squats, on Squat Day - speed deadlifts, on Overhead Press Day - speed bench, and on Bench Day - speed overhead press. So you still practice each competition lift weekly, even when the hard day for that lift uses a variation.

**Auxiliary** exercises are there to fix the weak points of the main lifts - [{Back Extension}] and [{Shrug}] for Deadlift, [{Lateral Raise}] and [{Skullcrusher}] for Press, [{Bulgarian Split Squat}] and [{Lying Leg Curl}] for Squat, [{Face Pull}] and [{Triceps Dip}] for Bench. Alsruhe calls them "the icing on the cake", since all the main stuff happens in the first 3 blocks. And if you have access to strongman equipment (farmer's walks, yoke, sandbag carries) - those are great options for auxiliary exercises too.

**Conditioning finishers** depend on the day: [{Battle Ropes}] intervals, light [{Thruster}]s, [{Jump Squat, Bodyweight}] and [{Kettlebell Swing}]s, each one is 10 minutes of intervals. Those are optional - Alsruhe says you can do those in the beginning, in the end, or just skip those.

## Set & Rep Scheme

**Strength block.** You gradually build to a rep max - 5, 3 or 1 reps, depending on the week. It should be a real max for that day, i.e. the heaviest weight you can do for those reps with good form. Each warm-up attempt before that counts as a separate giant-set round.

**Volume block.** 3 sets at 80% of today's top set. Reps depend on the rep max:

* After a 5RM: 3 sets of 10
* After a 3RM: 3 sets of 8
* After a 1RM: 3 sets of 5

The first volume set is AMRAP - a "strongman set". After the 5RM, Alsruhe does 12-15 reps there, and at least 10 reps for the following 2 sets. If you can't get there - that's okay! Just break it into smaller chunks, and still do all the reps.

**Why 80%:** in Alsruhe's words, "80% of anything is heavy enough that if you're doing something wrong it is going to tell you, and yet it is light enough that you shouldn't do a ton of things wrong." And it's 80% of the max set you hit that day, on that day's variation, not 80% of the all-time 1RM.

**Speed work.** You do a set of 3 every minute for 10 minutes (EMOM), with 50-70% of 1RM of the competition lift. Try to move the weight with max speed, but don't sacrifice form - all reps should look identical. If you have bands or chains - it's highly recommended to use those, but just regular weights are okay too.

**Assistance.** 3 sets of 10-15 with 60 seconds rest.

## Progressive Overload

There's no fixed weight increase - the rep maxes do the progression. As you get stronger, your daily 5RM, 3RM and 1RM go up, and the volume follows at 80%.

**Speed work** is the one written progression. It goes up 5% a week and resets at the start of each wave:

* 1st-4th week: 50%, 55%, 60%, 65%
* 5th-9th week: 50%, 55%, 60%, 65%, 70%
* 10th week (deload): 6x3, 50%

Weeks 1-4 are clearly visible on the author's whiteboard. The second 5-week block (going up to 70%) comes from runners, which lines up with the author's "somewhere between 50 and 70 percent." Don't go beyond 70% though - one runner tried it, so speed workouts turned into grinding.

**Testing.** Week 10 is a deload. After that, you test the maxes for 2 weeks (11 and 12) - on week 11 you do Deadlift and Bench Press, and on week 12 - Squat and Overhead Press. As Alsruhe puts it: "you're probably not going to hit a bench PR and an overhead press PR the same week, or a deadlift and a squat."

**When you're fried.** Drop assistance first. Like Alsruhe says: "If it is no longer going to assist you because you are so fried, then you can't do it, do not do it. The best thing you can do when you're in a hole: stop digging." The heavy, volume and speed blocks are the actual program, and assistance is optional if you're having a bad day.

**In the app:**

* For the rep max set, Liftosaur will ask you what weight you used. It suggests the weight based on the 1RM of the exercise, and it'll get more accurate over time - after each workout it updates the 1RM of the variation based on the top set.
* Once you're done with the rep max set, it'll use 80% of that weight for the 3 volume sets.
* The speed sets are based on the 1RM of the competition lift. On the test weeks, the new 1RM is taken from the weight you did there.

## How Long to Run It / What Next

Run the full 12 weeks. Runners report the first wave is the hardest - your body adapts to the giant sets by wave 2.

I wouldn't do it twice back to back. According to reviewers, Alsruhe recommends taking a break of several months before you do it the second time, and doing it at most 2 times consecutively. After this program, something simpler would feel pretty light, so it's a good moment to switch to something with steady progression - like [5/3/1 Boring But Big](/programs/the531bbb) or [nSuns](/programs/nsuns). If you want to keep focusing on conditioning, [Tactical Barbell: Operator](/programs/tactical-barbell-operator) pairs well with separate conditioning workouts. And if you liked the max-effort and speed days - [Westside Conjugate](/programs/westside-conjugate-method) is the parent system.

## Rest Times

* **Giant sets (strength and volume)**: ~15 secs between exercises - basically just enough time to walk over to the other station. After the conditioning piece you get a 90 sec break, and then you start another round
* **Speed work**: the timer starts a set each 60 secs, so your break is just the rest of that minute.
* **Auxiliary**: 60 secs
* **Max testing**: 3-5 minutes between attempts

## How to Pick Starting Weights

1. **Set 1RM for the 4 competition lifts** (tap on the exercise name, then 1RM). The speed sets use it from day 1. If you don't know the 1RM, you can use the rep max calculator there, with some recent hard set.
2. **The rep max sets suggest a weight based on the variation's 1RM**, but in the beginning it's just a guess, especially for variations you rarely do. So, increase the weight in small jumps, stop on the max weight you can still move with good form, and enter it. Leave 0-1 reps in the tank, and don't grind ugly reps.
3. **Start the giant set partners with moderate weights**, e.g. a moderate goblet squat, a bodyweight pull-up, a 20lb ball. You'll do 4+ rounds of each, in between the max attempts.
4. **Speed sets should look fast.** If the bar slows down with 50%, the 1RM is probably set higher than it should be, so lower it.

## Common Modifications

* **Variations**: you can swap any strength variation for something that targets a weak point, e.g. rack pulls for lockout, pause squats for the bottom, floor press or pin press for bench lockout. But stick with the same variation for the whole wave.
* **75% for volume**: if you keep missing the volume reps by a lot, you can drop the volume weight to 75% of the top set. A reviewer did exactly that and finished the program.
* **Skip the finishers**: the 10-minute conditioning is optional, so on long days you can just skip it. Don't skip the giant sets though.
* **Strongman assistance**: if the gym has farmer's handles, a yoke or a log, use them for auxiliary exercises. You can create those as new exercises in the app.
* **3 days a week**: the free version is 4 days, and Alsruhe says you "have to go four" with it. So if you can only do 3 days, probably better to go with a different program.

### Is Dark Horse good for beginners?

Brian Alsruhe says it's suitable for any level, since the weights are based on the daily rep max. But if you're a total beginner, you'd probably progress faster on a simple linear progression. Dark Horse works best if you know how to do rep maxes with good technique, and want more conditioning.

### How long does a Dark Horse workout take?

A workout would take around 75-90 min. The author allocates 30 min for the rep max, 15 for volume, 10 for speed sets, 20 for accessory work, and optionally another 10 for conditioning. If the gym is crowded and you need to set up several giant set stations, add another 20-30 min on top of that.

### How many reps should I do on Dark Horse speed work?

You do sets of 3, a set each minute, for 10 minutes. We begin from 50% and add 5% each week, and the percentage resets in the beginning of each new block. Each rep should be fast and look the same. If the bar slows down - the weight is more than it should be.

### What does 80% of the top set mean in Dark Horse?

The volume sets are 80% of the weight you got on the rep max set that day, on the same variation. E.g. if you got to 300lb for 3 on deficit deadlift, they'd be 240lb for 8 reps. It's not 80% of the all-time 1RM.

### Do I need to change exercises every week?

Nope - stick to the same variation for each lift for the whole 3-week block, and only switch it once the block is over. A lot of people change it each week, but then it's really hard to tell whether you actually get stronger.

### Can I run Dark Horse 3 days a week?

The free version is a 4-day program, and Alsruhe says it doesn't fit into 3. Each day is centered on a main movement, so you'd just lose a whole movement completely. So if you can only train 3 times a week - better go with another program.

### What if I can't finish the volume reps?

That's okay, especially in the first block. You can just split it into smaller chunks, and finish all the reps anyway. But if it happens like that every week - drop the volume weight to 75% of the top set.
