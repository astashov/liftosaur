A 12-week, 5-day bodybuilding program by Alexander Bromley (Empire Barbell) that hits every body part twice a week across three 4-week blocks. Block 1 volumizes with isolation work done first, Block 2 runs heavy pyramids on "underload" compound variations, and Block 3 finishes with reverse pyramids on "overload" variations. Loading is RPE-based and autoregulated - no percentages to calculate.

<!-- more -->

## Origin & Philosophy

KONG was created by strongman and coach Alexander Bromley of Empire Barbell, author of *Base Strength*. Bromley walks through the whole design in his [breakdown video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GuRM2EkMctU).

Bromley calls it an "anti-specialization program." It's inspired by the dense, varied training of lifters like Bill Kazmaier, Doug Young, and Roger Estep - a huge variety of rep ranges, pyramids that bury you in volume before the heavy work, and compounds done after priming exercises that pre-fatigue weak points. His argument: most lifters specialize too early, and the bodybuilding-style work they avoid is exactly what would take their physique and strength to the next level. Where his strength programs like [Bullmastiff](/programs/bullmastiff) put the barbell lifts first, KONG spends four weeks deliberately putting them last.

## Who It's For

- **Experience level**: Novice to intermediate (Boostcamp lists it as intermediate). You should know your way around a full gym.
- **Prerequisites**: Ability to gauge RPE honestly (the whole program is effort-based), and 5 free days a week
- **Primary goal**: Hypertrophy, with strength work in blocks 2-3
- **Cut/bulk**: Run it in a calorie surplus. Bromley pairs the progression with "a calorie surplus" and says not to try to cut on it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Every body part gets hit twice a week, with priority position rotating - arms, delts, and hamstrings get trained first while you're fresh, which is exactly backwards from most programs and exactly why lagging "extremity" muscles finally grow
- Huge rep-range exposure (singles to sets of 21) - Bromley's "broad and developmental" base means you get hypertrophy, work capacity, and heavy top sets in one 12-week run
- RPE-based autoregulation means no stalling and no test week required - bad days just mean less weight that day
- Three distinct 4-week blocks keep training interesting: pump work, then pyramids, then heavy reverse pyramids
- Well-reviewed: about 4.35/5 on Boostcamp, with reviewers reporting steady size and strength gains (one: "I finally have visible triceps")

**Cons**

- Serious time cost: 5 days a week at ~75 minutes a session, and some runners needed extra rest days beyond the two programmed ones
- No dedicated core work and no calf work - add your own if you care about either
- Needs a full commercial gym as written (leg press, hack squat, cables, machines) - home gym lifters will be substituting a lot
- No deload weeks - week 4 of every block is deliberately the hardest, and blocks roll straight into each other, so you manage fatigue yourself
- Expect brutal soreness in Block 1: leg extensions before squats and JM presses before incline pressing is by design, and your compound working weights will be humbling while pre-fatigued

## Program Structure

- **Split**: 5-day bodybuilding body-part split - Day 1: triceps/delts/chest, Day 2: posterior-chain lower, Day 3: delts/back/biceps, Day 4: quad-focused lower, Day 5: biceps/back/triceps
- **Periodization**: Block periodization with three 4-week blocks - Volumizing (isolation first, high reps), Pyramids (compounds first, "underload" variations), Reverse Pyramids ("overload" variations, heavy top sets)
- **Schedule**: Fixed weekly, e.g. Mon-Fri with weekends off, or spread the two rest days wherever recovery needs them
- **Order rotates on purpose**: the same muscle appears multiple times a week, but takes priority position one day and a back seat another

## Exercise Selection & Rationale

**Block 1 (Volumizing)** puts focused isolation work first - JM presses and French presses, front raises, hamstring curls, curls - because these are the movements that fix common weak points (triceps, side delts, hamstrings, biceps) and they're exactly what gets skipped when left to the end of a workout. Compounds like [{Incline Bench Press}], [{Squat}], and [{Romanian Deadlift, Barbell}] come last, pre-fatigued. Bromley: "if you haven't barbell squatted after doing leg extensions, all I can say is I'm sorry." The two lower days split priority - Day 2 starts with hamstring curls (posterior priority), Day 4 starts with leg extensions (quad priority). Day 3 even includes old-school 21s, a throwback to Arnold's *Encyclopedia*. Liftosaur has no JM press or French press, so this version uses [{Skullcrusher, Barbell}] and [{Triceps Extension, Barbell}] in their place.

**Block 2 (Pyramids)** flips the order: compounds first, now that Block 1 built the work capacity to use them. Each day is anchored by an "underload" variation - [{Seated Overhead Press}], [{Stiff Leg Deadlift}], [{Bent Over Row}], [{Bench Press Close Grip}], and close-stance high-bar [{Squat}]. These are deliberately harder, longer-range, more awkward versions of the main lifts, so they force lighter weights while building the muscle that supports the standard lifts.

**Block 3 (Overload)** swaps in "overload" variations built to handle *more* weight than your standard lifts: [{Push Press, Barbell}] (leg drive), block-elevated [{Deadlift}] (shortened range), [{Pendlay Row}], [{Bench Press Wide Grip}], and wide-stance low-bar [{Squat}]. Accessories carry over from Block 2 so you can keep progressing them.

Bromley programs by category, not sacred exercises - swap within the same muscle group if your gym differs (e.g. [{Chest Fly}] for cable crossovers, a different curl variation). Keep the underload/overload character of the Block 2/3 main lifts.

## Set & Rep Scheme

**Block 1 (Weeks 1-4)**: most exercises run the same climbing scheme - 2x15 at RPE 7 in week 1, 3x15 at RPE 7 in week 2, then 4x15 in week 3 ramping the effort across sets (RPE 5, 7, then two sets to failure). Week 4 is the hardest: drop to 10-12 reps, ramp to all-out heavy sets. Volume jumps 50% from week 1 to week 2, then intensity takes over. A few exercises use 20 reps instead of 15 (pushdowns, cable crossovers, hamstring curls, one-arm rows, Day 4 leg extensions), walking lunges use 12, and the curl 21s use 21. They keep that rep count through week 3 and join the 10-12 heavy scheme in week 4.

## Progressive Overload

KONG runs on RPE and rep targets, not percentages - "this is all autoregulated," per Bromley. Progression comes from three directions:

1. **Within each block**: sets are added (2→3→4 in Block 1), reps shift down while weight shifts up (12-10-8-5 → 10-8-5-3), and target RPE climbs (7 → 10). Every week has something that goes up.
2. **Weight at the same RPE**: as you adapt, the same "RPE 8" is more weight. Aim to jump weight whenever the prescribed RPE allows it.
3. **In the app**: weights are suggested from each exercise's 1RM via RPE tables, and the program calibrates that 1RM from your own answers. Every exercise's last set asks what the RPE actually was - your answer back-solves the 1RM, so next week's suggested weights match how the weight actually felt. On main lifts, the all-out AMRAP (reps + logged RPE) does a similar job, like the Doug Young-style autoregulation in [Bullmastiff](/programs/bullmastiff). A strong AMRAP raises the main-lift 1RM; a weak one keeps it, so if you set it too high, lower it by hand. You don't need to know any weights up front - the first week is the calibration week.

There is no failure protocol and no deload: if you miss a rep target, just hold the weight next week. If everything feels heavy for two straight sessions, take an extra rest day - recovery, food, and sleep are the pressure valves, because week 4 of each block is intentionally the hardest and blocks roll straight into one another.

## How Long to Run It / What Next

Run the full 12 weeks. The blocks build on each other - Block 1's capacity makes Block 2's pyramids productive, and Block 2's base makes Block 3's heavy sets safe - so don't skip Block 1 even though it looks "easy" on paper.

After a rest week, you can run KONG again with new weak points prioritized in Block 1, or rotate to a strength block now that you've built the base: [Bullmastiff](/programs/bullmastiff) is the natural Bromley follow-up, and [Jacked & Tan 2.0](/programs/gzcl-jacked-and-tan-2) or [The Rippler](/programs/gzcl-the-rippler) offer a similar build-then-intensify arc with more barbell focus.

## Rest Times

Bromley doesn't prescribe rest periods. Practical defaults that fit the ~75-minute sessions (and what the app uses):

- **Main lifts (Blocks 2-3)**: 3 minutes between pyramid sets
- **Everything else**: 90 seconds - Block 1 is high-rep pump work, so keep it moving

## How to Pick Starting Weights

There's no 1RM math to do up front - every set has an RPE target, and the program calibrates itself. Each exercise's last set asks what the RPE actually was; your answer back-solves that exercise's 1RM, so the suggested weights self-correct within a week or two. Three things matter:

1. **Start Block 1 lighter than your ego wants.** The first suggested weights are rough guesses - change them freely, and err light. RPE 7 means about 3 reps in reserve, and because you hit compounds pre-fatigued, your squat and incline weights will be well below normal - Bromley says that's "perfectly okay, the fatigue is the point."
2. **Answer the RPE prompts honestly.** Rating a set right after doing it ("how many more reps did I have?") is much easier than predicting a weight up front - that's the whole calibration mechanism. Sandbagging or ego-rating breaks it.
3. **Set the 1RM for the five Block 2-3 main lifts** (tap the exercise name → 1RM) when you reach week 5. If you don't know it, estimate from any recent hard set with the built-in rep-max calculator. A strong weekly AMRAP raises it; if you set it too high, lower it by hand.

## Common Modifications

- **Behind-the-neck press**: Bromley defends it ("very overblown as far as risk to reward"), but if your shoulders lack the mobility, swap in [{Overhead Press, Dumbbell}]
- **Painful exercises**: adjust weight or tempo, or swap the movement within the same muscle group rather than pushing through joint pain
- **Recovery limited?** Drop each exercise by one set before dropping a training day - the 2x/week frequency per body part is core to the program
- **Add core work**: 2-3 sets of [{Hanging Leg Raise}] or [{Ab Wheel}] at the end of the lower days costs 10 minutes and fills KONG's one real gap
- **Can't do 5 days?** KONG doesn't compress well into 4 days without wrecking session length. If you only have 4 days, run [PHUL](/programs/phul) or [Bullmastiff](/programs/bullmastiff) instead.

### Is KONG good for beginners?

KONG is written for novice and intermediate lifters who already know their way around a full gym. True beginners will grow faster on a simple linear progression like GZCLP or a basic full-body routine, and should build barbell technique before spending 5 days a week on a body-part split.

### Do I need to know my 1RM for KONG?

No. KONG runs on RPE and rep targets rather than percentages. The Liftosaur version suggests weights from each exercise's 1RM setting, then calibrates that 1RM automatically: every exercise's last set asks what the RPE actually was, and the answer adjusts the following week's weights. It still helps to set rough 1RMs for the five main lifts when Block 2 starts in week 5. A strong all-out AMRAP raises them from there.

### Can I run KONG while cutting?

Bromley says not to. He pairs the program with "a calorie surplus" and says the size and strength gains come "if you're not trying to like do a cut while you do this." Run it in at least a small calorie surplus.

### How long are KONG workouts?

About 75 minutes, 5 days a week. Block 1 sessions have the most exercises (up to 8) but short rests; Blocks 2-3 have fewer exercises but longer rests on the heavy pyramid work.

### Does KONG have deload weeks?

No. Week 4 of each block is deliberately the hardest week, and the next block starts immediately. The block transitions themselves change the stimulus enough to serve as a reset for most people, but if you're wrecked, take an extra rest day or an easy week between blocks.

### What are underload and overload variations in KONG?

Block 2 mains are "underload" variations - longer range of motion, more awkward, forcing less weight than the standard lift (close-grip bench, stiff-leg deadlift, high-bar close-stance squat). Block 3 mains are "overload" variations built to handle more weight than the standard lift (push press, block-elevated deadlift, wide-grip bench, low-bar wide-stance squat). You build muscle through a big range first, then teach it to move heavy loads.

### Can I swap exercises in KONG?

Yes - Bromley programs by category, not by fixed exercise. Swap any accessory for another movement hitting the same muscle group (his ebook does exactly that). Keep the character of the Block 2 and Block 3 main lifts though: an underload variation in Block 2, an overload variation in Block 3.

### What should I run after KONG?

Bromley's Bullmastiff is the natural next step - it takes the size and work capacity KONG built and converts it into barbell strength over 18 weeks. Running KONG a second time with different weak points prioritized in Block 1 also works well if size is still the goal.
