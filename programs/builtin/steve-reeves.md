It's Steve Reeves' full-body routine from the 1940s-50s. You do the same workout 3 times a week, with ~2 days off between workouts. Exercises are generally 3 sets of 8-12 - you choose a weight you can do ~8 times, and increase the number each workout. Once you hit 12 on all sets - you bump the weight and go back to 8. It hits all the muscles - presses, rows, curls, squats, deadlifts, and also the famous breathing squat + pullover superset. The workouts are pretty long though - close to 2 hours.

### Origin & Philosophy

Steve Reeves (1926-2000) won AAU Mr. America in 1947 and NABBA Mr. Universe in 1950, and then starred as Hercules in a 1958 Italian film. He's often considered to have the most symmetrical physique of the pre-steroid era - his arms, calves and neck measured about the same, and he had a tiny waist and wide shoulders.

Reeves trained full body, three times a week, his whole career. He wrote about it in his book *Building the Classic Physique the Natural Way* (1995). The routine here is what Reeves called his "Second Workout Schedule" - the intermediate routine his coach Ed Yarick gave him at his Oakland gym. The [Classic Physique Builder blog](http://classicphysiquebuilder.blogspot.com/2008/10/steve-reeves-intermediate-workout.html) reprinted it "without any changes, for historical purposes", and the same exercise list shows up on most "Steve Reeves workout" pages online, sometimes with small swaps.

The ideas behind it are simple:

- Full body each workout, so each muscle gets trained 3 times a week.
- Go through the muscle groups in the same order each time - shoulders, chest and back, arms, legs, lower back.
- Strict form and slow reps (e.g. 2 seconds lifting, 3 seconds lowering), no cheating.
- Each set should go til failure (or close to it), within 8-12 reps.
- Rest no less than a day and a half between workouts. E.g. Reeves trained on Monday morning, Wednesday evening, and Friday/Saturday morning.
- Focus on balance and proportions, not just size. He never did shrugs and side bends with large weights, since he thought they make the neck and waist look wider.

Reeves added sets over the years. According to the Classic Physique Builder blog, he did 1 set per exercise as a beginner, then 2, and then 3 once he was intermediate/advanced.

### Who It's For

- **Experience:** intermediate. Ed Yarick (Reeves' coach) gave it to Reeves as an intermediate program. You need to know how to squat, deadlift, bench and row with proper form, because in a single workout you push all of them close to the limit.
- **Goal:** muscle and proportions first, and strength is secondary. It's a physique-focused routine.
- **Bulk/cut:** it's better to run it while eating above maintenance, or at maintenance. 35 sets close to the limit in a single workout is a lot to recover from if you're in a calorie deficit.
- **Schedule:** you'd need about 2 free hours, 3 times a week.

If you're just starting, you can do the same exercises, but only 1 set of each instead of 3 - that's how Reeves started himself. Or you can pick some easier whole-body routine, like [Arnold's Golden Six](/programs/arnoldgoldensix) or [Basic Beginner Routine](/programs/basicBeginner).

### Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- You hit each muscle 3 times a week, which is great for growth if you're not advanced yet
- Simple progression - add reps each workout, and when you get 12 reps on all sets - bump the weight
- Balanced - presses/rows, curls/triceps, squats/hip hinges, so upper body doesn't have any big gaps
- Short rest times (~45 seconds) keep the pace high, plus you get a good pump
- Doesn't require special equipment - just a bar, dumbbells, a bench and a cable station (which is pretty easy to replace with something else)

**Cons:**

- Workouts are long - the app estimates ~110 minutes, and reportedly Reeves himself spent 2+ hours on each workout
- There're zero direct exercises for calves or abs in this version. Reeves had famous calves, so if you care about those - add some calf raises
- Lateral delts only get hit by the upright row, so a lot of people also add lateral raises there
- Squats and deadlifts come last in the workout, after 24 upper body sets. You'll be pretty tired by then, so you'll lift less on squats and deadlifts compared to a program where you do legs first
- Training each set until you can't do another rep 3 times a week is hard to recover from, unless your sleep and food are in order
- The breathing squat + pullover superset was supposed to "expand the ribcage". That won't really happen in adults though, so you can just treat it like a hard set of 20 reps

### Program Structure

It's a whole-body routine, and you do the same workout each time, 3 times a week, with a rest day (or more) in between (e.g. Monday, Wednesday, Friday). The program doesn't have weeks, phases or deloads - you just repeat the same workout, and the weights increase over time.

The order of exercises is: first dumbbell swing for a warmup, then shoulders, chest, back (one-arm row sits between bench press and flyes), triceps, biceps, legs + lats (supersets), and lower back is the very last.

### Exercise Selection & Rationale

- **[{Kettlebell Swing, Dumbbell}]** - warmup, to get the hips, lower spine and shoulders moving prior to the hard sets.
- **[{Upright Row, Barbell}]** - the first and the only direct exercise for shoulder width. Reeves cared a lot about V-shape (wide shoulders, small waist), and upright row hits side delts and upper traps. It's done with moderate weight for 15-20 reps, so it also warms up the shoulders for the presses.
- **[{Bench Press}]**, **[{Chest Fly}]**, **[{Incline Bench Press}]** - 3 chest exercises: a big press, a stretch, and an incline press for the upper chest. Incline press uses descending weight (i.e. lower weight on each set), so you'd stay within the 8-12 range even when tired. Some versions of this routine have dumbbell lateral raises instead of flyes - both are okay.
- **[{Bent Over One Arm Row}]** - main pulling exercise. Doing one arm at a time allows to stretch further and pull higher than with a regular two-handed bar row.
- **[{Triceps Pushdown}]**, **[{Bicep Curl, Barbell}]**, seated **[{Bicep Curl}]** - arms. Reeves did the second curl sitting on the edge of a bench, with dumbbells, so there's no way to swing the weight.
- **[{Squat}]** supersetted with **[{Pullover}]** - main leg exercise, paired with a lats/chest stretch.
- **Breathing squat + breathing pullover** - one set of 20 squats, with 2-3 deep breaths between reps, and then right away a light pullover, also with deep breaths. It's an old-school "chest expansion" superset, also used in [Super Squats](/programs/super-squats). Brutal set for both legs and lungs.
- **[{Deadlift}]**, **[{Good Morning}]** - 2 sets each, for the lower spine and hamstrings. Classic Physique Builder blog doesn't recommend good mornings, and says the routine is "just fine" without them. If they bother the lower spine - just drop them, or do a back extension instead.

### Set & Rep Scheme

- **Most exercises: 3 sets of 8-12 reps.** Pick the weight where you'd fail around 8 reps. It's a classic hypertrophy range - enough weight for strength, and enough reps for size.
- **Upright row: 3 sets of 15-20** with moderate weight. It's also kinda a warm-up for the shoulders.
- **Deadlift and good morning: 2 sets of 8-12.** Less volume for those, since the lower back is already tired after squats.
- **Breathing squat and breathing pullover: 1 set of 20.** Take the weight you'd normally squat for ~12-15 reps, and do deep breaths between reps - that slows the pace down, so you can get to 20.
- **Tempo:** ~2 seconds to raise the weight, 3 seconds to lower it, for each rep.
- **Effort:** go to failure on each set (or 1 rep short of it), with strict form. The last rep should still look the same as the first rep.

Technically the program doesn't have any AMRAP sets. But since you take each set pretty close to your limit within the 8-12 range, in practice each set is basically "do the max good reps you can, but not more than 12".

### Progressive Overload

This is double progression - first you add reps, then you add weight:

1. Pick a weight you can do for ~8 reps on all sets.
2. Each workout, try to beat the reps from the last time - e.g. 8, 8, 8 → 9, 9, 8 → 10, 9, 9, etc.
3. Once you hit **12 reps on all sets** (20 reps for the upright row and breathing squat), increase the weight next workout:
   - **+5lb** for upper body exercises, the breathing squat and good mornings
   - **+10lb** for squat and deadlift
4. With the new weight, you'll probably drop to ~8 reps again. And then just repeat.

In the app, each set has a range of reps - you just log how many reps you did, and if you reach the top of the range on all sets in a workout, the app automatically increases the weight for next time. If you didn't get to the top on any of the sets, the weight won't change.

If you can't do 8+ reps in a set with a proper technique, the weight is too heavy for you, and Reeves would reduce it. The app won't do that automatically though, so you'd need to reduce it by 5-10% manually.

The light breathing pullover doesn't progress - it's a stretch, not a strength exercise. The dumbbell swing warm-up doesn't progress either.

The program doesn't have a deload built in. If you feel beaten down for over a week, take a couple days of rest, or do a light workout with just 1-2 sets per exercise.

### How Long to Run It / What Next

You can run it for months - as long as reps or weights keep going up every week or two. Reeves trained full body like this for his whole career.

Signs it's time to change:

- You're stuck on the same weight and reps for most of the exercises for 3-4 weeks
- Workouts take so long you start skipping them
- You can't recover between workouts, and every workout you're weaker than the previous workout

After that, you may try a program with more volume per muscle, e.g. [PHUL](/programs/phul) and [PHAT](/programs/phat). If you're into the old-school style - there's [Arnold's Split](/programs/arnold-split). And if you want to get stronger on the big compound exercises, a program focused on that, e.g. [5/3/1: Boring But Big](/programs/the531bbb), would be a good fit.

### Equipment Needed

- Barbell, plates and a squat rack
- Flat and incline bench
- Dumbbells
- Cable station for pushdowns

For a home gym:

- **[{Triceps Pushdown}]** → [{Skullcrusher}] / [{Triceps Extension}] with a dumbbell
- **[{Incline Bench Press}]** → [{Incline Bench Press, Dumbbell}] if you just have adjustable bench and DBs

### Rest Times

- **Between sets:** about 45 sec. Sources say it's 45-60 sec.
- **Between exercises:** about 2 min. The Classic Physique Builder blog says Reeves rested up to 5 min when switching body parts in this routine.
- **Supersets:** zero rest going from squats to pullovers, same for breathing squats and breathing pullovers. You rest once you're done with the pullover.
- Breathing pullover, deadlifts and good mornings get 60 sec in the program, because those come immediately after the hardest sets of the day.

### How to Pick Starting Weights

Choose a weight you could do **about 8 reps with strict form, slowly**. If you're not sure about the weight, go a bit below what you think you could lift. Remember you're gonna do 3 sets with just 45 seconds between them, and by the time you get to squats you'd be pretty tired. You won't fully recover between sets, so on the 2nd and 3rd sets you'll likely do less reps compared to the 1st set. That's okay.

The weights in the program are just placeholders, so make sure to adjust them during the first workout. If the weight you chose is too light - no big deal, you'll quickly get to 12 reps, and it'll bump up the weight automatically.

For the breathing squat, use about 70-80% of your squat weight for the 8-12 sets.

### Common Modifications

- **Or try the 1951 "Classic Physique" routine.** In May 1951 Reeves published another whole-body routine, in *Your Physique* magazine. It starts with incline dumbbell presses, and there's also a breathing front squat, seated barbell curls, lunges ("splits") and calf presses on the leg press. It's 1-3 sets per exercise, with 12-15 reps on many of them. So it's the same idea, just with different exercises.
- **Add calves and abs.** At the end of the workout, do 2-3 sets of [{Standing Calf Raise}] plus some [{Hanging Leg Raise}] or [{Crunch}].
- **Swap flyes for lateral raises** to hit side delts more. Some versions of the routine do that.
- **Drop good mornings** if they bother the lower back.
- **Cut volume if you're short on time.** Do 2 sets instead of 3 on the arm and chest isolation exercises. That saves ~8-10 minutes, and each muscle is still trained 3 times a week.
- **3-day split versions** you may find online (e.g. chest and back on Monday, shoulders and arms on Wednesday, legs on Friday) are modern adaptations. Reeves did the same whole-body workout each time.

### What was Steve Reeves' workout routine?

Steve Reeves did one full-body workout three times a week, with at least a day and a half of rest between sessions. His intermediate routine has 14 exercises after a dumbbell swing warm-up, mostly 3 sets of 8-12 reps: upright rows, bench press, one-arm rows, flyes, incline press, triceps pushdowns, two kinds of curls, squats with pullovers, breathing squats with pullovers, deadlifts and good mornings.

### How many days a week did Steve Reeves train?

Three days a week, full body every time. He liked a day and a half of rest between sessions, e.g. Monday morning, Wednesday evening and Friday or Saturday morning.

### How does progression work in the Steve Reeves workout?

Choose a weight you can do about 8 times with strict form. Every workout, try to beat the number of reps from the previous workout. When you hit 12 reps in all sets, add 5lb (10lb for squat and deadlift), and start again from about 8 reps.

### Is the Steve Reeves workout good for beginners?

The 3-set version of the routine is way too much for a lot of novices - it's ~35 hard sets, and it'd last close to 2 hours. Reeves himself started with 1 set per exercise, and added sets once he got further along. So if you're just starting, you can do the same exercises, but only 1 set each.

### How long does a Steve Reeves workout take?

A workout would take like 1.5-2h if you use a 45s timer after each set. Reeves apparently took 2h or even more. If you only do 2 sets for the aux exercises, it'd save you like 10 minutes.

### What are breathing squats and do they work?

Breathing squat is basically 20 reps, where you take 2-3 deep breaths between reps, and then immediately do a light pullover, also with deep breaths. Old-school bodybuilders believed it expands the ribcage. For adults that's not really the case, but those 20 reps are super hard anyway, and are really good for leg size and conditioning.

### Did Steve Reeves train to failure?

Mostly yes - the weight was chosen so you could do 8 reps, and all sets were done til failure (or 1 rep short of it), with strict form. And without cheating reps just to lift more weight.
