The Jailhouse Strong prison workout is a bodyweight-only routine by powerlifting coach Josh Bryant and Adam benShea, built from the rep schemes inmates use after weight piles were pulled out of US prisons. Three 30-minute sessions: an upper body day (a 1-to-10-to-1 pull-up pyramid and a "Deck of Pain" of push-ups), a lower body day (Tyson squats and descending "Jailhouse" lunges), and a total body day (Juarez Valley burpees). You need a pull-up bar and nothing else. Each workout has a small starting version and a full version, and you move up once you finish every set.

## Origin & Philosophy

Josh Bryant is a powerlifter with some records under his belt and a powerlifting coach, and Adam benShea is a co-author and work partner of Josh. Josh and Adam published the book [Jailhouse Strong](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1492755796) in October 2013. It collects workout methods they saw in prisons after most US state prisons banned weights at the beginning of the 1990s. Inmates had no barbells, so they got strong and conditioned using bodyweight, a deck of cards, and TONS of reps.

The program is based on the "Prison Workout: The Jailhouse Strong Routine" article, which was first published on Bodybuilding.com, and then on the [Jailhouse Strong blog](https://www.jailhousestrong.com/blog/2018/03/16/prison-workout-the-jailhouse-strong-routine). It's a sample week the authors put together themselves, using the approaches from the book. All those approaches are basically ways to split a huge number of reps, so you could keep going even when you're tired:

- **Pyramid** - you increase reps 1, 2, 3... till you reach the top number, then go back to 1. The easier sets at the bottom work like a rest, so you don't need to stop.
- **Deck of Pain** - you flip a card, and do that many reps. You never know what card is next, so it's hard to pace yourself.
- **Jailhouse Method** - you choose some number, and do one rep less each set, until you're at 1. It's hard in the beginning, and becomes easier towards the end.
- **Juarez Valley Method** - odd sets count from the top number decreasing, even sets count from 1 increasing (20, 1, 19, 2, 18, 3...). The name comes from Cereso prison in Ciudad Juarez, Mexico, and "valley" is the shape of the reps. Each pair of sets adds up to the same number, which keeps the load even through the whole thing.

The idea is pretty simple - you can do it wherever you are, and "it all comes down to how hard you push yourself". A workout should take 30 minutes or less. You record how far you got, and then on the following workout you try to do another set.

## Who It's For

- **Experience level**: Late beginner to intermediate. The authors mention that a complete bodyweight beginner probably won't be able to do the full workouts initially, so this version starts all exercises on a smaller scale.
- **Prerequisites**: You should be able to do 1-2 strict pull-ups minimum, and 10-15 push-ups. If you can't do a pull-up yet - check the modifications below.
- **Primary goal**: Muscular endurance, work capacity and conditioning, plus some muscle growth from all that volume. One full cycle (all three days) is 100 pull-ups, 380 push-ups, 200 squats, 210 lunges per leg and 210 burpees. It won't build max strength like a barbell program with big weights would.
- **Cut/bulk/maintenance**: Best for a cut or maintenance - lots of reps burn a lot of calories, and sessions are short. Also a good option for travelling or a hotel room, when you can't go to a gym.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Only needs a pull-up bar. No gym, no weights - so you can do it in a hotel room, a garage, a park, etc.
- Workouts take about 30 minutes, the full upper body day is designed to fit within 30 minutes max.
- Very high weekly volume - 100 pull-ups and 380 push-ups in a single day, which builds endurance in the upper back, chest and arms pretty quickly.
- Strength and cardio within the same workout. Short rests and hundreds of reps get the heart rate really high.
- Clear goals. Each workout has a fixed number of reps, and burpee day also has time targets (under 24 minutes is good, under 20 is excellent, under 15 is elite), so you can clearly see the progress.

**Cons**

- Leg loading is pretty low. 200+ reps of bodyweight squats and lunges is more of an endurance thing than building leg strength/size.
- Nothing hits hamstrings, calves and core directly, except the bit they get from the burpees and lunges.
- That much push-up volume in a single day can irritate elbows and wrists if you jump straight into the whole deck.
- The upper day is just pull-ups and push-ups - zero overhead pressing and zero rowing.
- Progression stops when you reach the complete versions. After that, you'd need harder variations (pistol squats, weighted pull-ups) / faster times to keep progressing.

## Program Structure

- **Split**: Upper / lower / total body. Day 1 is pull-ups and push-ups, Day 2 - squats and lunges, Day 3 - burpees.
- **Schedule**: The original is 3 days on, 1 day of rest, and then repeat, so you end up working out about 5 times in a 7-day week. You can also do a fixed Monday/Wednesday/Friday schedule if you need more rest. The authors even mention you could do the workouts twice a day if you have time, but that's only for people who are used to that volume.
- **Periodization**: Not really, at least not in the classic sense. You keep adding volume until you get to the full workout, and then you try to finish it faster. The rep schemes (pyramid, deck, descending ladder, Juarez Valley) change how the reps are distributed, not the intensity.

## Exercise Selection & Rationale

- **[{Pull Up}]** - the only pulling exercise in the program. Works lats, upper back and biceps, and balances all that push-up volume. If you prefer, you can do chin-ups or other grips instead - the article allows that.
- **[{Push Up}]** - main pushing exercise, for chest, front delts and triceps. It's simple to do for very high reps, which is why it's used for the Deck of Pain.
- **[{Squat, Bodyweight}]** - Tyson squats. Legend says Mike Tyson did this card drill to keep his legs in shape while in prison. It's a high-rep knee-dominant exercise for quads and glutes.
- **[{Lunge, Bodyweight}]** - single-leg exercise after the Tyson squats. Each leg works separately, so it's harder than a regular squat for the same number of reps, and hits the glutes more.
- **[{Burpee}]** - total body day. It's the 5-count burpee from the article: drop into a crouch with hands on the floor, kick the feet into a plank, return to the crouch, jump as high as you can, and land in the starting position. Hits legs, chest, shoulders and core, and it's the conditioning part of the program.

Upper and Lower workouts don't overlap, so you can do them on back-to-back days. The burpee day hits a bit of everything - it's more like a conditioning day to wrap it all up.

## Set & Rep Scheme

Every exercise uses a fixed rep scheme with no added weight:

- **Pull-ups - pyramid**: you go 1, 2, 3... up to 10, then down to 1 again. That's 19 sets, 100 reps total. If you're a novice, authors recommend to do a pyramid of 4 or 5 instead (16 or 25 reps). Try to finish it in 15 minutes.
- **Push-ups - Deck of Pain**: 52 cards, one set per card. Number cards are their value, face cards are 10, aces are 11. Whole deck is 380 push-ups, authors recommend to aim for under 15 minutes. You don't need real cards - the app shuffles the deck at the start of every workout, so the order is new each time. Half deck is a fixed set of 26 cards, i.e. 190 push-ups.
- **Squats - Tyson squats**: 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3... 9, 9, 10, so 19 sets, 100 reps. Complete version is 2 rounds one after another, 200 squats total, "preferably without resting" between rounds.
- **Lunges - Jailhouse Method**: you choose a number, then do one rep less each set until you hit 1. Jailhouse 10 is 55 reps, Jailhouse 15 is 120 reps, Jailhouse 20 is 210 reps. Note that reps in the app are per leg, so Jailhouse 20 means 210 lunges for each leg.
- **Burpees - Juarez Valley**: Juarez Valley 20 goes like 20, 1, 19, 2, 18, 3... 11, 10 - so 20 sets, 210 reps. There're also smaller versions - Juarez Valley 10 (55 reps) or Juarez Valley 15 (120 reps).

There's no AMRAP set and no RPE target. The reps are fixed, and once you're at the full version you progress by finishing them faster.

Note that the article doesn't specify which Jailhouse number to use for the lunges - it mentions Jailhouse 20 and Jailhouse 30 as examples. Here it goes up to Jailhouse 20, since combined with 200 squats it's quite a lot.

## Progressive Overload

Each exercise has a ladder of set schemes, and you begin with the first set scheme there. If you finish all sets with all reps, the app switches you to the following scheme for the upcoming workout. If you miss any reps, you keep the same set scheme and try it again.

- **Pull-ups**: pyramid of 4 -> 5 -> 6 -> 7 -> 8 -> 9 -> 10.
- **Push-ups**: half deck (190 reps) -> full deck (380 reps).
- **Squats**: one round of Tyson squats (100 reps) -> two rounds (200 reps).
- **Lunges**: Jailhouse 10 -> Jailhouse 15 -> Jailhouse 20.
- **Burpees**: Juarez Valley 10 -> Juarez Valley 15 -> Juarez Valley 20.

When you get to the complete version, the goal becomes time. You need to finish pull-ups and deck within 15 minutes each, and get burpees under 24, then 20, then 15 minutes. After that, authors suggest switching to harder variations, e.g. pistol squats instead of bodyweight squats. Similarly you can switch to weighted pull-ups, diamond push-ups or jump squats.

The program doesn't have a scheduled deload. On the Art of Manliness podcast, Bryant suggests doing "natural deloads" - like, if you don't feel it that day, go easier, or even skip it completely. For bodyweight programs, he recommends a deload about every 8 weeks. If you feel tired sooner, take an extra day off, and also you can go a step lower on the ladder for a week.

## How Long to Run It / What Next

For most people, climbing the ladders usually takes 4-8 weeks. After that, you can just stick with it and continue cutting time. It's also a good travel program to do between gym blocks.

When bodyweight exercises stop making you stronger, you may want to switch to a program where you lift a bar. [GZCLP](/programs/gzclp) and [Basic Beginner Routine](/programs/basicBeginner) are both good first programs for that. Or, if you want to stay with bodyweight but focus on getting stronger vs endurance, check out [r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine](/programs/recommended-routine). It uses harder exercise progressions for 5-8 reps, not hundreds of reps.

## Equipment Needed

- **A pull-up bar** - a doorway one, a playground, or even a sturdy tree branch is fine.
- **A deck of cards** - optional, the app shuffles a deck for you every workout. If you prefer to flip physical cards, the reps you log won't match the listed sets, so the app won't switch you from half deck to full deck. You'd need to switch it manually in that case.
- **About 16 feet of space** for walking rest between lunge sets.

If you don't have a pull-up bar, you can do inverted rows beneath some sturdy table, or towel rows on a door. It's not exactly the same exercise, but it still trains the same movement.

## Rest Times

The program barely has rest:

- **Pull-ups**: 30 sec. The low-rep sets in the beginning and the end of the pyramid are kinda active rest anyway.
- **Push-ups**: 15 sec - about how long it takes to flip another card.
- **Tyson squats**: 10 sec - just enough to move a card. No break between both rounds.
- **Lunges**: 20 sec - after each set, you walk 8 feet across the room, and then 8 feet to return, like the article describes.
- **Burpees**: 10 sec - between sets, you walk about 8 feet away, and then 8 feet to return to where you were (5-10 steps). Bryant does the same walk for the Juarez Valley method.

If you can't do all sets with these timers - take more time between sets, but still do all reps. And on following workouts, try to gradually reduce that time.

## How to Pick Starting Weights

There's no weight to pick - every exercise is bodyweight. You pick your starting step on the ladder instead.

By default, everyone starts with the smallest version of the program: a pyramid of 4, a half deck, a single round of Tyson squats, Jailhouse 10 and Juarez Valley 10. If you've been doing bodyweight workouts for a while and that's not challenging enough for you - you can skip ahead before starting. Just tap on an exercise in the program, and choose a later set variation.

Simple test - if you can do 12+ strict pullups, begin a pyramid from 7 or 8. If you can do 40+ push-ups in a row, go with a whole deck. But don't jump to whole versions of everything right away - 380 push-ups and 100 pullups in a day is a big jump even for experienced lifters, and soreness could ruin a whole week after that.

## Common Modifications

- **Can't do a pull-up yet**: for the pyramid, you could do [{Negative Pull Up}] (jump to the top, then slowly lower yourself for 3-5 seconds) or band-assisted pull-ups, and stick with a pyramid of 4 until you're able to do proper reps.
- **Push-ups are hard**: for the deck, start with [{Incline Push Up}] on a bench or a table.
- **Want it harder**: the authors suggest the "Tyson pistol squat workout" - same card pattern, just with [{Pistol Squat}]. You could also swap in [{Jump Squat, Bodyweight}] for the Tyson squats, weighted pull-ups for the pyramid, or [{Diamond Push Up}] for the deck.
- **More core**: add 3 sets of [{Hanging Leg Raise}] at the end of the upper day. The Art of Manliness version of the prison workout has hanging leg raises as one of the Deck of Pain suits.
- **Mixed Deck of Pain**: in the classic variant, every suit gets a different exercise. E.g. clubs are push-ups, spades are pull-ups, diamonds are squats, and hearts are leg raises.
- **Less volume**: do it on Monday/Wednesday/Friday, instead of 3 days on and 1 rest day.

### What is the prison workout?

It's a bodyweight routine built from the training methods inmates use when they have no weights. This version is from Josh Bryant and Adam benShea, the authors of Jailhouse Strong. It has three 30-minute sessions: pull-ups and push-ups, squats and lunges, and burpees. All you need is a pull-up bar.

### What is the Deck of Pain workout?

You shuffle a deck of 52 cards, and flip cards over, card by card. Each card is a set - for number cards you do as many reps as their face value, for face cards - 10 reps, for aces - 11 reps. So the whole deck is 380 reps. The Jailhouse Strong routine uses push-ups for that, and you try to get through the deck in less than 15 minutes.

### What is the Juarez Valley method?

It's a rep scheme where odd sets go down from the top number, and even sets go from 1 and increase. E.g. Juarez Valley 20 goes 20, 1, 19, 2, 18, 3, etc, until 11, 10 - 210 reps total. Each pair of sets sums to 21, which makes the workout about equally hard from beginning to end.

### Is the prison workout good for beginners?

The full version is way overkill for the majority of novices - there're 100 pull-ups and 380 push-ups in a single workout. If you're new, the authors recommend to start with a lighter version, e.g. with a pull-up pyramid of 4 or 5. So, this program starts each exercise with a lighter version, and bumps it to the next version after you finish all the reps.

### Can you build muscle with the prison workout?

Yes, especially lats/traps, chest and arms, since the reps are super high there. Legs won't grow that much though, because bodyweight squats and lunges get too light pretty quickly. If you want bigger legs, you can move to pistol squats, or add some squat program with a bar on top.

### How many days a week should I do the prison workout?

In the original you train 3 days in a row, then rest 1, so it's about 5 workouts a week. If that's a lot, you can just do the 3 workouts on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Each workout is about 30 minutes.

### What is the Tyson squat workout?

It's a card drill credited to Mike Tyson, from back when he was in prison. You do squats like 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, etc, up to 9, 9, 10 - which is 100 squats total. In the Jailhouse Strong routine you do it 2 times back-to-back, without a break, so you end up with 200 squats.
