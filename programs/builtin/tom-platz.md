The 1980s training split of Tom Platz, "The Golden Eagle", the bodybuilder known for the best legs of his era. Each workout trains one body part with lots of sets taken to failure. Chest and back share a day (morning and evening), then shoulders, arms and legs, then a rest day. Leg day alternates. One week is high-rep squats, done as a warm-up ladder and two all-out sets of 15-20 reps. The other week is hack squats and leg extensions instead. For advanced lifters who want big legs and can recover from a lot of volume.

## Origin & Philosophy

Tom Platz competed in Mr. Olympia seven times, from 1979 to 1986, and his best result was 3rd place in 1981. People called him "The Golden Eagle" and "The Quadfather", and even today his thighs are kinda a benchmark of leg development for bodybuilders. In 1992 he did a squat contest with powerlifter Fred Hatfield, and did 525lb for 23 reps! He also co-wrote *Pro-Style Bodybuilding* (1985) with Bill Reynolds.

Platz explained his leg training in detail in an [Ironman interview](http://ditillo2.blogspot.com/2008/11/tom-platz-on-squatting.html) and in a [leg training manual](http://ditillo2.blogspot.com/2022/01/tom-platz-leg-training-manual.html). As a young lifter he squatted twice a week - one heavy day of 2-8 reps and one rep day with two sets of 15-20 reps, adding 5lb a side each workout. After he moved to California, from 1978 on he trained legs once a week and squatted only every other week. After a squat day he "couldn't even think about coming back in the gym and training legs for another 10 to 12 days". In later interviews he said that training two times a day, six days a week with Arnold made him smaller.

So, the idea was super simple - train less often, but push every session past the moment when you can't do another rep. For almost all exercises it was forced reps, partials, negatives and isometrics with a partner. Except squats - there it was just regular sets til you can't do any more reps, going all the way down, like "my butt almost touches the floor".

The upper body split is based on the 1980s routine that was reprinted by [The Barbell](https://thebarbell.com/tom-platz-workout/) and [Fitness Volt](https://fitnessvolt.com/tom-platz-profile/) - chest in the morning and back in the evening, then shoulders, arms and legs, and then a rest day. Those are later reconstructions, not Platz's actual logs, so the exact number of sets is approximate.

## Who It's For

- **Experience level**: Advanced (3+ years). You should know squat 1RM, be able to squat below parallel with good form, and be able to do 18-33 sets per workout.
- **Primary goal**: Hypertrophy, with additional focus on legs.
- **Cut/bulk**: Bulk or maintenance. Platz himself did the most extreme versions (30-50 rep squat sets) 3-4 months before a contest, but he was eating tons of food and could recover from it. If you're on a calorie deficit, it's going to be really hard to recover from this volume.
- **Not for**: Beginners, lifters who train alone and want to take all sets to absolute failure, and people with cranky knees - deep high-rep squats and deep hack squats stress the knees a lot.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Squats are only on alternating weeks, like Platz did, so you can push super hard on both rep sets and still recover
- 6 sets of lying leg curls on each leg day - that's more direct hamstring volume than most bodybuilding splits have
- Calves are trained on each leg day, with both straight-leg ([{Standing Calf Raise, Leverage Machine}]) and bent-knee ([{Seated Calf Raise, Leverage Machine}]) variations
- Squat progression is automatic - if you hit the rep targets, the app adds 10lb to the squat 1RM, which bumps up all the sets
- All the other exercises use double progression, so you always know when to add weight
- Each body part has 5 days to recover before it's trained again (5 workouts, then a day off)

**Cons**

- Huge volume. Back day is 29 sets and shoulder day is 27 sets plus abs. Back, arm and shoulder days take 85-120 minutes
- Each muscle is trained once every 6 days (once every 5 days if you do chest and back on the same day like Platz) - lower frequency than modern hypertrophy programs recommend
- Chest and back were trained the same day (morning and evening). Most people can't train twice a day, so here they are separate workouts, which makes it 5 sessions per cycle
- Needs a full gym - Smith machine, pec deck, hack squat, leg extension, leg curl, two calf machines, cable station
- No hip hinge (deadlift or Romanian deadlift) at all, matching Platz's own training. The back and glutes get less work than in most programs
- Platz's rep ranges for pull-ups (15-30) and lat pulldowns (20-40) are very high. This version starts pull-ups at 10+ and pulldowns at 20-30, and most people will still need a band or a light weight
- Platz's intensity techniques (forced reps, partner-resisted negatives) need a training partner

## Program Structure

- **Split**: Bro split, 1 muscle group per workout - Chest, Back, Shoulders + Abs, Arms, Legs
- **Periodization**: None. Everything is double progression, except Squat - there you progress by hitting a rep target
- **Schedule**: Rotating. Platz did 4 days on, and then 1 free day: chest (AM) + back (PM), shoulders, arms, legs, free day. If you can't do 2 sessions a day - just split chest and back into separate days, and take the free day after legs
- **Cycle**: 2 weeks. Upper workouts are the same in both weeks, only legs are different:
  - **Week 1 - Squat workout**: [{Squat}], [{Lying Leg Curl}], calf work
  - **Week 2 - machine workout**: [{Hack Squat}], [{Leg Extension}], [{Lying Leg Curl}], calf work

In the app, a "week" is just the 5 workouts of one cycle. It doesn't have to be 7 days.

## Exercise Selection & Rationale

**Chest** starts with incline press, then two fly movements, and then weighted dip at the end. [{Incline Bench Press, Dumbbell}] targets the top part of the pecs, which was lagging for a lot of 1980s bodybuilders. [{Chest Fly}] (dumbbell version) gives the pecs a good stretch at the bottom, and [{Pec Deck}] has constant tension at the top. And then [{Chest Dip}] with added weight, to completely exhaust the pecs. Platz was doing them til he couldn't do any more reps, with drop sets.

**Back** starts with a big chunk of vertical exercises - [{Pull Up}] and [{Lat Pulldown}], for width. For the pulldowns, Platz would just go for like 20 minutes nonstop, not even counting sets, and a partner would help him with the last reps. Then [{T Bar Row}] and [{Seated Row}] for thickness. And [{Pullover}] (dumbbell) - it hits the lats with locked arms, and also stretches the ribcage.

**Shoulders** start with [{Shoulder Press, Smith Machine}] for the front delts, then three lateral delt movements: [{Lateral Raise}], [{Lateral Raise, Cable}] (one arm at a time) and [{Upright Row, Barbell}]. Rear delts get [{Reverse Fly}] (bent over with dumbbells) and [{Reverse Fly, Leverage Machine}]. Abs are on this day too: [{Crunch}] and [{Hanging Leg Raise}] (Platz used a Roman chair).

**Arms** go in order: biceps first, then triceps, then forearms. For biceps there's [{Bicep Curl, Barbell}] to build size, [{Incline Curl}] to stretch the long head, and [{Bicep Curl, Leverage Machine}] to finish them with constant tension. For triceps - [{Triceps Pushdown}], [{Bench Press Close Grip}] and one-arm [{Triceps Extension}] (dumbbell, overhead). And [{Wrist Curl}] for forearms, with high reps.

**Legs, week 1** is all about [{Squat}]. High-bar, feet shoulder width or narrower, toes pointed a bit to the sides, and you go as deep as you can. The day is pretty short on purpose - those sets are super hard, so more quad exercises on top would just make it harder to recover. Then - [{Lying Leg Curl}], and then some calf work.

**Legs, week 2** replaces the squats with machine exercises. [{Hack Squat}] was another one of Platz's staples - heels close together, toes pointed to the sides. And closer to the end of his career, Platz would also rise onto his toes and push the hips forward (sissy-style), to target the lower quads. [{Leg Extension}] isolates the quads.

**Substitutions**:

- [{Shoulder Press, Smith Machine}] → [{Overhead Press, Dumbbell}] / [{Overhead Press}]
- [{Pec Deck}] → [{Chest Fly, Cable}]
- [{Hack Squat}] → [{Hack Squat, Smith Machine}] / [{Front Squat}]
- [{Reverse Fly, Leverage Machine}] → face pull, and also you could just do another set of [{Reverse Fly}]

## Set & Rep Scheme

- **Squat**: 4 warmup sets with increasing weight (15 reps with 35%, 10 with 50%, 10 with 60%, 5 with 75% of 1RM), and then 2 AMRAP sets - first is 15+ reps with 65% of 1RM, second is 20+ reps with 55%. That's how Platz described it - warmups 135x15, 225x10, 315x10 and 405x5-6, and then "about two sets that we counted" of 15-30 reps.
- **Compound presses, rows and squat machine exercises**: 10-15 / 10-20 reps.
- **Isolation exercises**: 10-20 reps (10-15 for upright rows, machine rear delt flyes, pullovers and barbell curls). Lateral raises and Smith presses start from 12.
- **High-rep exercises**: [{Lat Pulldown}] - 20-30, single-arm [{Triceps Extension}] - 15-30, [{Wrist Curl}] - 20-30.
- **Calves**: 10-15 for both calf raises.
- **Pull-ups and dips**: AMRAP sets. For pull-ups you should aim for 10+ (sources mention 15-30, so if you can do less than that - use a band / a partner). For dips - 10+ with added weight.
- **Abs**: [{Crunch}] 2x100 and [{Hanging Leg Raise}] 4x25.

The majority of sets are done til you can't do another rep (or pretty close to it) - that's how Platz trained. If you have a partner, add 1-2 forced reps in the last set of each isolation exercise. For leg curls, Platz's manual says "no more than two forced reps".

Platz liked doing high reps for legs, since he thought quads respond to long sets. In the Ironman interview Platz said he wouldn't stop a set until he could "feel a muscle begin to tear from the bone". You don't have to go that far though - just stop when you can't keep a good technique anymore.

## Progressive Overload

**Squat**: the weights are percentages of your squat 1RM. If you get at least 15 reps on the first AMRAP set and at least 20 on the second, the app adds 10lb to your 1RM. That raises all 6 sets for your next squat day. If you miss either target, the 1RM stays the same and you try again in two weeks. This matches Platz's manual: raise the weight by 5-10lb once you break your rep record.

**Everything else**: double progression. Every exercise has a min and max number of reps, e.g. 5x10 within 10-20 reps. If you successfully finish all sets with the required number of reps, next time the app will add 1 rep to every set. And when you hit the top of the range, it'll bump up the weight and reset the reps to the bottom.

- **5lb** for most exercises
- **10lb** for [{Hack Squat}]
- **2.5lb** for [{Lateral Raise, Cable}]

**Pull-ups and dips** are AMRAP sets. For [{Chest Dip}], you add 5lb once you get 45 total reps in all 3 sets. For [{Pull Up}] - it adds 5lb once you do 120 total reps in all 8 sets (i.e. 15 per set).

**Stalls**: if you can't add reps for 3 workouts straight, reduce the weight 10%, and then slowly build up to the old weight again. Platz didn't do scheduled deloads, he just had long rest periods between leg days. So if you feel beaten and exhausted - just rest an extra day.

## How Long to Run It / What Next

Platz's manual says to stay on a routine "for at least three months" before changing it. Run this for 12-16 weeks (about 8-10 cycles).

Move on when most exercises stop progressing, or when your knees or lower back can't take more high-rep squats.

## Rest Times

The sources don't give fixed rest times for the upper body. These are defaults:

- **Squat warm-up sets**: 90 sec
- **Squat AMRAP sets**: 4 min. Platz mentioned taking "four or five minutes" of rest between sets before a high-rep squat set, and in the manual it says you should wait "until you feel mentally ready"
- **Compound exercises** (presses, rows, pulldowns, pull-ups, dips, hack squat): 90 sec
- **Isolation exercises**: 60 sec

## How to Pick Starting Weights

**Squat**: enter 1RM for Squat in the app, and the program will calculate all the weights based on it. If you don't know 1RM, take the weight you can do 5 clean reps with, and multiply it by 1.15. Be honest here - if you enter 1RM that's higher than it really is, the 65%x15 will feel like a max effort from the very first workout.

**Everything else**: there're default weights in the program, but you probably want to change those to a weight you can do for the min reps, leaving 1-2 reps in reserve. Lots of exercises go to 20 reps, so pick a smaller weight than you'd use for a regular 8-12 reps program.

**Typical pitfall**: starting high-rep exercises with way bigger weights than you should. E.g. if you do 8x20 lat pulldowns / 8x12 Smith press with the weight you'd usually use for 10, by the end you'd barely manage like 4-5.

## Common Modifications

- **Squats on all leg days**: if you're younger / recover fast, you can use Platz's earlier approach. Week 1 is the squat day as written, and on week 2 you'd also add a heavy squat day (5-6 sets of 2-8 reps) right before the hack squats.
- **Cut the volume**: just do the low end of each set range (e.g. 3 sets instead of 5, 4 sets of lat pulldowns instead of 8). For the upper body days that'd be about a third fewer sets.
- **Add a hip hinge**: in case the posterior chain needs it, add [{Romanian Deadlift}] 3x8-12 to the pulling day (or to the week 1 leg day).
- **Train with a partner**: Platz did forced reps, partial reps after failure, and partner-resisted isometrics on leg curls and extensions. Only do those on the last set of the exercise, not on every set.
- **Pre-contest leg extensions**: Platz mostly added leg extensions in the weeks before contests. Outside contest prep you can swap those for another set of [{Hack Squat}].

### What was Tom Platz's workout split?

In the 1980s he used to train 4 days in a row, and then take 1 day off. First day was chest in the morning and back in the evening, then shoulders, arms, legs, and then a day off. Abs could be added to any day. This program uses the same order, but splits chest and back into separate days - for people who can't train twice a day.

### How often did Tom Platz train legs?

Early in his career, squats were twice a week - a session with big weights and a high-rep session. Starting from 1978, legs got trained weekly, and squats only showed up every other week. On the alternating leg workouts it was hack squats, leg extensions and leg curls instead. So that's why the program has a 2-week cycle.

### Is the Tom Platz workout good for beginners?

No, it's not for novices. You have 18-33 sets per workout, most of the sets are done til failure (i.e. until you can't do another rep), and for squats you need to do 15-20+ reps below parallel with proper form. Novices would recover way better on a program with lower volume, that teaches the main compound movements first. So I'd do e.g. GZCLP for a year, and only after that switch to it.

### What was Tom Platz's squat routine?

He'd do warmups like 135x15, 225x10, 315x10 and 405x5, and then about 2 "counted" rounds of 15-30 reps, all the way til he couldn't do another rep. The most famous one is 525lb for 23 reps, in 1992. The program uses the same structure, but with percentages of 1RM instead - 4 warmups, and then 2 AMRAPs with 65% and 55%.

### Can a natural lifter do Tom Platz's leg workout?

Yes, but with less volume, and without going past failure on each set. Platz trained legs only one time in 7-12 days, because his workouts were super brutal. The leg workouts here are way shorter than Platz's famous 40+ set sessions, and squats are only every other week. So it's realistic for advanced natural lifters.

### Why are there no deadlifts in this program?

Platz didn't do much hip hinge stuff. For legs it was just squats, hack squats, leg extensions and leg curls. So if you want some deadlifts/Romanian deadlifts for lower back and glutes, you can add 3 sets of those on back day.

### How long are Tom Platz workouts?

Chest and leg days take around 50 minutes. Back, arms and shoulders days are waaay longer - 85-120 minutes, because you do 8 sets each of pull-ups, lat pulldowns and Smith presses, and also 300 reps of abs on the shoulders day. Leg day on week 1 is short, but it's the hardest one of all.
